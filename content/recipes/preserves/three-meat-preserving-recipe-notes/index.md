These three preparations combine pork or beef with simple seasonings.

**Important:** These are ingredient and preparation notes, not validated canning recipes. The boiling-water preservation methods accompanying these preparations are not safe for storing meat at room temperature and are intentionally omitted.

Meat is a low-acid food and requires a tested pressure-canning recipe for shelf-stable preservation. A sealed lid does not prove that the contents are safe. Adding vinegar, salt or oil does not make an untested process safe.

#### 1. Pork Shoulder or Neck with Bacon

##### Ingredients

The following amounts are given per jar, but the jar capacity and weight of meat are not specified. They cannot be treated as a validated preservation formula.

- Pork neck or shoulder, preferably a fattier cut — quantity not specified
- 1 tablespoon vinegar with 9% acidity per jar
- 1 teaspoon salt per jar
- 2 thick slices white bacon or pork fat per jar

##### Preparation notes

1. Cut the pork into small pieces.
2. Cut the white bacon or pork fat into thick slices.
3. The preparation combines the pork with the vinegar, salt and bacon.

**Cooking and preservation:** No validated pressure-canning process is provided. The boiling-water treatment is omitted here. A safe conventional cooking time is also not specified, so this is not a complete ready-to-cook recipe.

#### 2. Pork Belly with Garlic and Bay in Brine

##### Ingredients

- Fresh pork belly — quantity not specified
- Peppercorns — quantity not specified
- Garlic, chopped — quantity not specified
- 1 bay leaf per jar

**For the brine:**

- 1.5 litres water
- 150 g salt

##### Preparation notes

1. Cut the pork belly into small pieces.
2. Chop the garlic.
3. Combine the water and salt in a saucepan and bring to the boil to dissolve the salt.
4. The preparation combines the pork belly, peppercorns, garlic and bay leaves with the hot brine.

**Cooking and preservation:** This brine is not a validated curing or preservation formula. No safe pressure-canning process or conventional cooking method is provided, so this is not a complete ready-to-cook recipe.

#### 3. Beef with Onion, Garlic and Olive Oil

##### Ingredients

- 1.5 kg beef, cut into small pieces
- 1 onion, chopped
- Garlic, chopped — quantity not specified
- Olive oil, for frying — quantity not specified

**Additional ingredients used during the original jar-packing stage:**

- 15 g salt per 500 g meat
- Additional olive oil, enough to cover the meat — quantity not specified

The jar-packing salt and oil quantities are recorded for completeness, not as a recommended seasoning or preservation method.

##### Cooking method

1. Cut the beef into small pieces.
2. Chop the onion and garlic.
3. Heat olive oil in a pan.
4. Add the onion, garlic and beef.
5. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the beef is cooked through. The stated cooking time is approximately 40 minutes, but the heat level, piece size and cut of beef are not specified; time alone does not establish doneness or tenderness.

**Preservation:** Do not cover the cooked beef with oil and treat it as shelf-stable. The original jar-processing stage is omitted because it is not a validated safe canning method.

#### Safe Storage

- For ordinary cooked versions, refrigerate promptly in shallow containers or freeze for longer storage.
- Do not leave cooked meat to cool slowly in a large pot of water.
- Do not rely on reheating to rescue improperly preserved meat.
- If meat has already been stored at room temperature using an unvalidated method, do not taste it to check whether it is safe.
- For shelf-stable meat, use a tested pressure-canning recipe that specifies the ingredients, packing method, jar size, headspace, processing time and pressure appropriate to your altitude.
